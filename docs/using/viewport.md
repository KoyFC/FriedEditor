# The viewport

The viewport shows the scene without running it. Left click selects a node, and a press anywhere inside the selected node's box drags it, even where one of its children is drawn over it, the wheel zooms, and the right or middle button pans.

A scene that has a `Node3D` opens in 3D, and the toolbar's 2D and 3D switch between the two views. In 3D, left click selects the node whose box is nearest, the wheel moves the camera closer or farther, the middle button pans, and holding the right button makes the camera a free one: moving the pointer turns it about its own position and W, A, S and D fly it (E up, Q down, Shift faster). The selected node has an arrow for each of its x, y and z, along its parent's axes, and dragging one changes only that field in a single undo step; Rotate and Scale are for 2D, and a 3D node's rotation and scale are edited in the inspector. F turns the view to the selected node, the ground grid has the x axis in red and the z axis in blue, and each camera shows the edges of what it sees out to its far plane (the selected one in yellow).

The toolbar's Move, Rotate and Scale buttons (W, E and R, shown by their icons and named when the pointer rests on them) put handles on the selected node, where it is in the world even when it is a child of another node, and dragging one is a single undo step. Move has an arrow for each axis, ending in a triangle, and a square between them that moves both at once.

The viewport draws at the size of its panel, so nothing is scaled, and the white frame is the area the game shows on the selected platform: the display's size divided by the game camera's zoom there. The platform selector previews that platform's screen: the camera area follows the project's display mode on it, and the Issues tab checks the assets for it.

Each scene remembers its own view: the mode (2D or 3D), where the camera is, its zoom or distance and angles, and which node was selected. Opening the scene again, or the editor, puts it back as it was left, and a scene that was never opened starts from the default view. The editor keeps these per project in its configuration folder, `state.json`, and also which nodes of the hierarchy are folded.

In 2D the toolbar has a grid: the first button shows or hides the grid drawn over the scene, with a stronger line every fourth square and wider squares when the view is zoomed out so the lines do not crowd, and the field sets the size of a square in world pixels. The second button snaps what is dragged: a move to the squares, a rotation to 15 degrees and a scale to tenths. Holding Ctrl while dragging does the opposite of what the button says, so a node can be placed freely while snapping is on and snapped while it is off. The grid is only drawn by the editor, the game does not have it, and its settings are remembered per project.

Three buttons float in the corner of the view. The first brings the selected node to the middle (the F key does the same, in 2D and in 3D, and in 2D it zooms out when the node is larger than the view), the second resets the view to how a scene first opens, and in 2D the third shows the zoom and a click on it sets it to 100%, the game's own pixels.
